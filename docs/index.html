Altheastix eBay Order Manager, a Tampermonkey userscript

eBay picking, packing, and shipping at scale

eBay tells me what sold, not which warehouse box the sticker is in, what envelope it needs, or what I've already packed and marked as shipped. A 6,700-line Tampermonkey userscript fixes that, and cut my daily order processing from 45 minutes to about 12.

the job

10-15 orders a day. Every one of them is a physical object in a numbered box.

Altheastix ships around 350 items a month, mostly stickers. An order is a trip to a container, a count, a ziplock, an envelope, a decision about postage, and a walk to the mailbox. The eBay page where you find out what to go and get, to then pack and ship.

This is why the SKU on the card matters so much: B04 is not an identifier, it's a drawer and a position in the box. The panel isn't a list of orders. It's a picking list.

The saving isn't any single feature. It's that a batch of fifteen stops requiring fifteen separate rounds of attention, and more quietly, that the batch no longer needs re-checking afterwards to find out what actually went out.

Why eBay's own page doesn't work for thisshow

eBay's page buys postage. I needed one that runs a pick-and-pack.

Each eBay order gets a weight box, three dimension boxes, a service dropdown and an insurance radio, adding to a total you pay at the top. For ~$5 stickers none of that matters: shipping is always one ounce, and most of the times the same envelope.

The two fields that decide what I physically go and fetch, SKU and quantity, are 12px grey under the title. Everything else about packing is absent. BUT: almost all info is there to make it a great tool for picking, packing, and shipping.

What packing needsWhat eBay's page gives
Which box the sticker is in SKU, 12px grey, under the title
How many to count out “Qty: 3”, same grey, same size
One list of everything to pick A pick list, per order
Printable addresses for a home printer None. Labels are bought, not printed
Which orders need a manila envelope None. Open each one and read it
Orders that arrived since the page loaded None. The page is a snapshot
What's already packed None
Weight and dimensions Six inputs per order
Insurance Two radios per order

The pick list was the one thing on the page shaped like the job. The rest is a rebuild of that page, in the browser, around the eight steps above.

what was built

The rebuilt page, part by part.

Same URL, same orders, same eBay session. The script replaces the layout in the browser. Click a marker.

1

1 · picking

The SKU is a shelf address.

Stickers are stored in lettered boxes. F24 is the 24th sticker in box F. Sorted alphabetically, the SKU list becomes a walking route: open box A once, take everything from it, move on.

F24
box Fcontainer on the shelf
sticker 24position in that box

Suffixes carry the packing rule:

  • E59B Bitcoin ×3 three of one SKU
  • B01D 5in Lg oversized, different envelope
  • M02 set, manila envelope
  • F01 🇨🇦 Canada, international stamp
  • A07* over $25, goes out as an eBay label with tracking

The panel lists one pill per order line, sorted by SKU, so every box is visited once and a pill always points at exactly one order. A pill reading ×3 means count out three of that sticker for that buyer. Grouped by letter, click any to jump to its order card. The filter matches SKU, buyer name and item title. Packed orders strike through rather than disappearing, so the list doesn't shift while you work down it.

Colour is physical, not decorative. An order with two or more SKUs takes its own colour from a 40-colour palette, and every one of its pills in the panel carries that colour, so the pills that go into the same envelope read as one group even when they sit in different boxes. A green wash means one SKU, two or more of it, and the amber ×2 pill says the same thing in the panel: the single most common packing mistake. A thick yellow border means a large sticker and a different envelope; orange means manila. A bold yellow * at the end of a pill means that order clears the $25 threshold and ships with a tracked label — the same yellow as the highlighted total on its card.

2 · new orders, no reload

The page is a snapshot. It checks for new sales in the background.

eBay's page shows the orders that existed when it loaded. A sale landing twenty minutes into a packing session is invisible until you reload, and reloading mid-batch loses selections, half-typed address edits, and any automation in flight.

So the script polls instead. Every 5 minutes it asks eBay's own awaiting-shipment endpoint for the current list of order ids and compares them to the ones captured at load.

SKUs to Pick and Pack (15)
· checked just now ·

The pill only appears when there is news. It never reloads on its own. Clicking it is the reload. Try a failed poll: the status line goes amber rather than staying quiet, because a watcher that is silent when it's broken looks identical to one that has nothing to report.

Never reloads by itselfAutomation may be in flight and an address may be half-edited. The pill is an offer.
Refuses mid-batchPaused while a batch ship or an automation tab is running.
Counts additions onlyAn id leaving the list means shipped or cancelled, never a reason to interrupt.
Silent on anything inconclusiveA sign-in redirect or a changed response shape backs off to 15 minutes instead of raising a false alarm.
Shows its heartbeat· checked 2m ago · check now, amber if a check failed or none has succeeded in two intervals. Not a countdown; a per-second timer turns an ambient check into something you watch.
v3.26–4.36 three kinds of postage

Every order is a hand-addressed envelope, an international one, or an eBay label with tracking.

The split comes from the order total against one threshold. Above $25 the margin carries a tracked label; at or under, it goes out as a plain #10 envelope and the buyer is told why in the message.

Derived from price rather than remembered, so it stays consistent across the four places it appears: the +tracking link, the highlighted total, the bold yellow * on the order's SKU pills, and the Select standard envelope filter.

eBay gives one print dialog per envelope. Print All Envelopes consolidates the batch into a single print window, one envelope per page, scaled to #10. Custom Envelope takes a pasted address block, parses it into editable fields, and prints a one-off. Envelope history keeps what was printed and what eBay confirmed as shipped, so a card printed yesterday says so before you print it again.

v3.34–3.43 the address as data to be linted

A stamped envelope is an expensive place to discover a bad address.

Every address is checked against structural rules before it can be printed: line count, a buyer name that isn't a number, a street line that starts with a house number (unless it's a PO Box), a valid City ST 12345 line, and a real state or territory code, all 59 of them, including the military ones.

Canada is checked separately: postal-code format and all thirteen province codes. Other international destinations are skipped. Too many valid formats to lint reliably.

One rule exists for an eBay quirk: a line after the name containing only digits means eBay split the street number from the street name, duplicating it.

The real validateAddress() function, ported verbatim. Edit it, or load a broken sample.

v4.23–4.35 a batch you can walk away from

Forty orders, one at a time, each waiting for a real outcome before the next begins.

Orders run one at a time, because the message tab takes focus, so parallel runs fight over the browser. A pre-flight dialog states the order count, message count and estimated duration (30s per order, measured).

Ready
0 shipped · 8 to go

Run it. One of the eight fails to ship; one ships but fails to message. At the end the rescue sweep retries the un-messaged card once, in the background, and reports the result.

  • One attempt per card, per batch. The marker is written before the tab opens, so a crash or re-render can't loop into mailing the same buyer twice.
  • An unknown draft action is skipped, not defaulted to auto-send.
  • Two failures ends it, and the card keeps its pill for you to finish.
  • Retries open in the background; the manual Retry button opens in the foreground.
v4.15–4.23 what the buyer is told

A thank-you note that reads like a person wrote it, sent exactly once.

The real modal, verbatim. Three drafts live in the dropdown: Late + Gift, Late, no gift and Preorder Sticker. The buyer's first name comes from the order; eBay hands over ALL-CAPS names and WILL becomes “Will”, because a machine-looking greeting undoes the point of writing one. Every field you type lands in the preview in green as you type, an empty field shows as a pill rather than a gap, and the preview itself is editable: touch it and template sync stops, an “edited by hand” flag appears, and what you send is exactly what you see.

The automatic thank-you that rides along with a shipment is built the same way. The product noun and every pronoun agree with the quantity. The tracking sentence disappears entirely above the threshold. And “tomorrow” rolls to Monday if it would land on a Sunday, because nothing ships on a Sunday.

Sending it turned out to be the hard part

Sending it reliably took four fixes, all timing: the composer button clicked before React had hydrated it, the textarea grabbed before its iframe bound a submit handler, Send clicked once while it merely looked enabled, and nothing verified the outcome before the tab closed itself.

Now every step retries, and the tab only closes on positive evidence that the message went out. If it can't confirm, it stays open with the draft intact and a red banner explaining what went wrong.

Retry can never double-send

This is the constraint that shapes the whole messaging design. The “will ship” note and the thank-you message are one-shot side effects guarded by per-order flags, so retrying a failed shipment never mails the buyer twice.

The draft is read and deleted separately, with the delete happening only once the text is in the box, so a failed attempt leaves the draft intact and Retry sends the same message rather than an empty one.

the other half

Once it could act on its own, it could be wrong about its own work.

Everything above is layout and picking. Below is what changed when the page started marking orders shipped and messaging buyers by itself.

v4.21 the failure with no channel

Marking an order shipped opens a background tab. Sometimes that tab dies.

Mark as Shipped & Msg fires off a background tab that talks to eBay, waits for the order page, clicks through the flow and closes itself. It works nearly every time. For a long stretch of this project's life, the times it didn't were invisible:

a card whose ship tab died sat forever showing a green ✔ over the words “Marked as Shipped” while the favicon counter dropped as if the order had gone out. CHANGELOG.md · v4.21

The tab counter also counted the pending overlay, so a silent failure made the batch look more finished than it was.

Fixing that took five versions, and it changed how everything after it is built. A feature that can act on its own has to be able to say so when it fails.

v4.21–4.25 three honest states

A card is pending, confirmed, or failed. Never a fourth thing wearing the face of the second.

Pending is an amber spinner. Confirmed is green and only arrives when eBay says so. Failed is a red banner quoting eBay back, with the tab left open.

v4.24–4.25 how it knows

Confirmation is a positive signal, not the absence of a button.

The first version waited 12 seconds for eBay's “Mark as shipped” button and, if it never appeared, concluded the order had already shipped, which is also what a slow page, a markup change or a logged-out session look like.

It now reads eBay's progress stepper. eBay renames that step rather than restyling it: “Ship by Aug 26” becomes “Shipped Aug 23”. The match anchors on the ship prefix and lets the icon decide, accepting either of two independent signals. When neither is legible the answer is unknown.

The failure message quotes the evidence: eBay still shows this order as NOT shipped (eBay shows “Ship by Aug 26”). You are told what the tool saw, not what it concluded.

v3.79–4.28 the tab as a dashboard

You are not looking at the browser. You are at the table with a ziplock bag.

The count of remaining work is drawn onto the favicon and prefixed to the tab title: eBay's own icon with a translucent white box across the bottom-right at 65% of its width. When the batch is done the box becomes a green check.

diagnostics

Every risky subsystem ships with a report, a dry run, and a simulator.

Each drives the UI into any state without contacting eBay, so a failure banner can be checked without breaking a real order.

The dry run caught a real bug: cards becoming permanently unshippable because two code paths removed the ship button and nothing put it back.

what this says about how I work

      

        I build for the job on the table, not the screen.
      

      

        Every feature here started with a physical step: a walk to a box, a count, an envelope,
        a stamp. The screen is only the part where you find out what to go and get, so the page
        was rebuilt around the walk rather than around eBay's data model.
      

      

        I made the automation honest before I made it faster. A tool that acts on its own has to
        be able to say when it failed; the five versions spent on that one green checkmark changed
        how everything after it was built, and every risky subsystem now ships with a report, a
        dry run and a simulator.
      

      

        I used whatever tool removed the most drudgery at each stage, from StackOverflow and a text
        editor to ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok and now Claude Code with GitHub for versioning, while keeping
        the design decisions, the scope and the order of work my own.
      

      

        And I shipped small: 112 versions to a single user, me, each one the smallest change that
        removed the day's most tedious step. That is how a print button became a 6,700-line
        pick-and-pack system without ever being planned as one.

Two companion scripts

      

        Both cover jobs that happen outside the bulk shipping page, and both install on their own.
      

      

        

          
Offer Msg Picker eBay active listings

          
Ten marketing message templates in the Send Offer modal, with the discount read from the offer form itself rather than hard-coded, so a message can never promise a discount the offer doesn't send. Mounts as a sibling of eBay's React container, because mounting inside it meant the character counter re-created the textarea on every keystroke and stole focus.

        

        

          
Print Address from Gmail mail.google.com

          
A 🖨 Print address button on Etsy and eBay order emails that prints a #10 envelope from the Gmail tab itself. Parses the address out of the rendered message, because Gmail's sanitiser strips <address> entirely. No innerHTML anywhere. Gmail enforces Trusted Types, which made an earlier version throw a CSP violation and do nothing at all.
